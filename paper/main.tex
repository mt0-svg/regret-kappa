\title[Doubly uniform regret in dimension one]{The constant of doubly uniform regret in one-dimensional online linear regression is three}
\author{\authorlink}

\begin{abstract}
We determine the leading constant of the minimax regret of online linear regression in dimension one when the features are revealed one at a time, with no bound known in advance, and the regret is measured against every linear predictor, with no bound on its parameter. For outcomes bounded by $B$ and a horizon $T\ge355713$, both known to the learner, the minimax regret lies between $B^2(3\log T-2\log\log T-15.2)$ and $B^2(3\log T+0.36+o(1))$, so it is $(3+o(1))B^2\log T$. The upper bound is the regret of an explicit deterministic learner, built on a Gaussian mixture potential; the lower bound is forced by an explicit randomized adversary, whose features can be taken in $[0,1]$. With the features known in advance the constant is $1$, so revealing them on the fly triples it; this answers, in dimension one, a question of Gaillard et al.~(ALT 2019). Lean~4 proves both bounds with their constants.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

We determine the leading constant of the minimax regret of online linear regression in dimension one when the features are revealed one at a time and the regret is measured against every linear predictor: it is $(3+o(1))B^2\log T$ for outcomes bounded by $B$ and horizon $T$, both known to the learner.

In each round an adversary reveals a feature $x_t\in\R$, the learner predicts $\yh_t$, and the adversary reveals an outcome $y_t\in[-B,B]$; the regret compares the square loss of the learner with that of the best $\theta x_t$ in hindsight. No bound on the features is known in advance and none is put on the comparator $\theta$, so a bound on the regret is uniform over the comparators and over the features, \emph{doubly uniform} in the terms of \citet{GGHS}. In dimension one, \citet[Theorem~5 and Proposition~6]{CQRZ} showed that the minimax regret is of order $\log T$, and in dimension $d\ge2$ that it is linear in $T$ \citep[Theorem~7]{CQRZ}. Our main result gives the constant, with explicit bounds on both sides. For an integer $T\ge3$ let
\begin{gather}
j_0=\lceil\log(3\log T)\rceil,\qquad L=2\log\frac{T}{20e\,j_0},\qquad k_0=T-\lfloor T/2\rfloor,\notag\\
c_1=\frac12+\frac43-\frac8{\pi^2}+\log(\pi^2)+\frac{\pi^2/4-2}{\pi^2e^2}=3.3186327\ldots,\notag\\
b(T)=\bigl(1-e^{-j_0}\bigr)\Bigl[L+\log\frac{k_0}{(\sqrt L+2)^2}-c_1-\frac{\log k_0}{2k_0}-\frac1{k_0}\Bigr],\label{eq:bT}\\
U(T)=2\log\Bigl(e^2+\frac{(\sqrt T+1)(3T+2e^{-1/2})}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Bigr).\notag
\end{gather}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:main}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.main}
\mapnote{Lean states the theorem as \lname{RegretKappa.MainTheorem}, with the minimax regret in the extended real numbers, and takes the limit from \lname{RegretKappa.kappaEqThree}, a first formal proof of the limit with cruder constants.}
Let $B>0$, and consider online linear regression in dimension one in which, at each round $t=1,\dots,T$, an adversary reveals a feature $x_t\in\R$, with no bound known in advance, the learner predicts $\yh_t\in\R$, and the adversary reveals an outcome $y_t\in[-B,B]$; the learner knows $B$ and $T$, and the regret $\Reg(T)$ is measured against the best $\theta x_t$ in hindsight with $\theta\in\R$, on which no bound is put \textup{(}Section~\ref{sec:setting}\textup{)}. For every integer $T\ge355713$ the minimax regret $\Regs_T(B)$ of deterministic learners satisfies
\[
B^2\bigl(3\log T-2\log\log T-15.2\bigr)\le B^2\,b(T)
\le\Regs_T(B)\le B^2\,U(T).
\]
Consequently $\Regs_T(B)/(B^2\log T)\to3$ as $T\to\infty$.
\end{theorem}

The upper bound is the regret of an explicit learner (Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}), and
\[
U(T)\le3\log T+2\log\frac{3}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt T}+\frac{0.81}{T}+\frac{12.35}{T^{3/2}},\qquad 2\log\frac{3}{\sqrt{2\pi}}=0.35934751\ldots;
\]
the lower bound is forced by an explicit randomized adversary (Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}). At $T=10^6$ the theorem gives
\[
22.629622\,B^2\le\Regs_{10^6}(B)\le41.807880\,B^2 ,
\]
where $U(10^6)=41.807879\ldots$ and $b(10^6)=22.629622\ldots$, with $j_0=4$, $L=2\log(10^6/(80e))$ and $k_0=5\cdot10^5$. Randomized learners do no better: the adversary forces the same bound on their expected regret (Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded} and Remark~\ref{rem:rand}).

The adversary needs only features in $[0,1]$ and outcomes in $\{-B,0,B\}$ (Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded}). This bears on a question of \citet{GGHS}. When the features are known to the learner from the start, they proved that the minimax regret in dimension $d$ is $dB^2\log T+O(\log\log T)$, with an explicit learner and a lower bound for features in $[0,1]^d$ \citep[Theorems~7 and~4]{GGHS}, and they asked the following.

\begin{question}[{\citealp[Section~4.1]{GGHS}}]\label{q:GGHS}
``The open question is therefore whether we can improve the lower bound and make it larger for strategies that only discover the features on the fly, or if a doubly uniform regret upper bound of $dB^2\ln(T)+\mathcal O_T(1)$ over the $\mathbf u$ and the $\mathbf x_t,\,y_t$ is also possible in the case of sequentially revealed features.''
\end{question}

Here $\mathbf u\in\R^d$ is the comparator and the features satisfy $\|\mathbf x_t\|\le X$. In dimension one, Theorem~\ref{thm:main} and Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded} answer it: the lower bound can be made larger, by a factor $3$ in the leading term, and no bound $B^2\log T+O(1)$ holds uniformly when the features are revealed on the fly. Our lower bound holds against every learner, whatever it knows; our upper bound uses $B$ and $T$. For the lower bound, \citet{GGHS} suggest random features that are dependent over time, or features built as functions of the strategy's past predictions. The adversary of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} does the first: its features depend on the past outcomes, and it never looks at the predictions.

\subsection*{Previous work}
With features revealed on the fly and a comparator penalized by $a\|\theta\|^2$, the forecaster of \citet{Vovk01} and \citet{AW01} has constant $1$: in dimension $d$, with $|y_t|\le Y$ and features whose coordinates are bounded by $X$, its square loss exceeds $\inf_\theta\bigl(\sum_t(\theta\cdot x_t-y_t)^2+a\|\theta\|^2\bigr)$ by at most $dY^2\log(1+TX^2/a)$ \citetext{\citealp[Theorem~1]{Vovk01}; \citealp[Theorem~5.6]{AW01}}, and \citet[Theorem~2]{Vovk01} proved a matching lower bound $(d-\varepsilon)Y^2\log T-C$. The penalty and the bound $X$ fix a scale. \citet[Theorem~15]{MHvE} gave a forecaster that needs no bound on the outcomes, with leading constant $1$ and a bound that grows with $\|\theta\|$ and with the norms of the features. For linear losses in dimension $d\ge2$, for every algorithm and every $\beta\ge0$ there are a sequence and a comparator $u$ with $\|u\|_S=\beta$, where $\|u\|_S^2=\sum_t(u\cdot x_t)^2$, against which the regret is at least $\beta\sqrt{T/2}$ \citep[Theorem~6]{Kot17}; so a bound that depends on $u$ only through this norm, which is invariant under linear changes of the features, grows like $\sqrt T$; this is a counterpart of the linear growth found by \citet[Theorem~7]{CQRZ} for the square loss. Our potential $\Gamma$ integrates the exponentials $e^{\theta\rho-\theta^2/2}$ against a weight in $\theta$, as the method of mixtures does for self-normalized sums \citep[Theorem~2.1]{dlPKL}; there the weight is a Gaussian of fixed scale, here it is $|\theta|^{-1}+2|\theta|^{-3}$ on $|\theta|\ge1$, read on a scale-free state $\rho$.

\subsection*{Method}
Both halves are read on the scale-free state $\rho_t=S_t/\sqrt{V_t}$, with $S_t=\sum_{i\le t}x_iy_i$ and $V_t=\sum_{i\le t}x_i^2$. The regret equals $\sum_t(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)+\rho_T^2$, and each round moves $\rho$ to $\rho\sqrt{1-s}+\varepsilon y\sqrt s$, where the adversary chooses the step $s=x_t^2/V_t$ freely; the same map, with the step fixed by the round, is that of the discrete analysis of \citet[Section~3]{HLPR} for two experts, in the variables $\rho\sqrt{1-s}$ and $\sqrt s$. The constant $3$ splits as $2+1$. The learner plays against the potential $2\log(C(\lambda+\Gamma(\rho)))$, where $\Gamma$ is a mixture of $e^{\theta\rho}$ against the weight $e^{-\theta^2/2}(|\theta|^{-1}+2|\theta|^{-3})$ on $|\theta|\ge1$; mixability of the square loss \citep{Vovk98,HKW,Vovk01} controls small steps, monotonicity of $\Gamma$ controls large ones, and each round costs a constant increment of $\lambda$, which gives $2\log T$, while the terminal condition $C(\lambda+\Gamma(\rho))\ge e^{\rho^2/2}$ for $|\rho|\le\sqrt T$ forces $C$ of order $\sqrt T$, which gives $\log T$. The adversary first drives $\rho_t^2$ up to about $2\log T$ with fair random signs, against which no prediction gains anything, and then holds the state until round $\lfloor T/2\rfloor$; by the regret identity this leaves the term $\rho_t^2\approx2\log T$ in the regret. Then it draws a parameter from a prior centred at the least squares estimate, of width $1/\sqrt{V_t}$ at the end of the first phase, and generates the outcomes from it: by the van Trees inequality \citep{GL}, estimating this parameter costs every learner about $\log T$ more.

\section{The game and the regret identity}\label{sec:setting}

This section fixes the game and rewrites the regret as a function of one real state, $\rho_t=S_t/\sqrt{V_t}$, which both strategies of the paper read.

\subsection{The game}
Fix $B>0$ and an integer $T\ge1$, both known to the learner. In each round $t=1,\dots,T$ the adversary reveals $x_t\in\R$, the learner predicts $\yh_t\in\R$, and the adversary reveals $y_t\in[-B,B]$. A \emph{deterministic learner} $\mathcal L$ is a sequence of maps $\mathcal L_t\colon\R^t\times\R^{t-1}\to\R$, and it predicts $\yh_t=\mathcal L_t(x_1,\dots,x_t;y_1,\dots,y_{t-1})$. On a play $(x,y)=(x_1,\dots,x_T;y_1,\dots,y_T)$ its regret is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:regret}
\Reg(T)=\sum_{t=1}^T(\yh_t-y_t)^2-\inf_{\theta\in\R}\sum_{t=1}^T(\theta x_t-y_t)^2,
\end{equation}
the regret $\Reg_\Theta(T)$ of \citet[Section~2.1]{CQRZ} with $d=1$ and $\Theta=\R$. Neither $\theta$ nor the features are bounded. The minimax regret is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:minimax}
\Regs_T(B)=\inf_{\mathcal L}\ \sup_{x\in\R^T,\ y\in[-B,B]^T}\Reg(T),
\end{equation}
the infimum over deterministic learners, taken in the extended real line. An adaptive adversary chooses $x_t$ and $y_t$ as functions of everything revealed before; against a fixed deterministic learner it produces a single play, so the supremum in \eqref{eq:minimax} is also the supremum over adaptive adversaries.

\emph{Randomized learners.} A randomized learner is a probability space $(\Omega,\mathcal F,\mu)$ with a deterministic learner $\mathcal L^\omega$ for each $\omega\in\Omega$, such that for each round $t$ and each history $(x_1,\dots,x_t;y_1,\dots,y_{t-1})$ the prediction $\mathcal L^\omega_t(x_1,\dots,x_t;y_1,\dots,y_{t-1})$ is a measurable function of $\omega$. Let $\Reg^\omega(T)$ be the regret of $\mathcal L^\omega$ on a play. Since the comparator $\theta=0$ has loss $\sum_ty_t^2$, $\Reg^\omega(T)+\sum_ty_t^2\ge0$, and the expected regret on the play is
\[
\E\,\Reg(T)=\int_\Omega\Bigl(\Reg^\omega(T)+\sum_ty_t^2\Bigr)\mu(d\omega)-\sum_ty_t^2\in(-\infty,+\infty],
\]
the integral of a nonnegative measurable function, with no integrability assumed. A deterministic learner is a randomized learner whose family is constant, and its expected regret is its regret. An adversary that ignores the predictions and plays the play $(x^i,y^i)$ with probability $w_i$, for $i$ in a finite set $I$, independently of the learner's randomness, is an adaptive adversary of a special kind; against it the expected regret is defined in the same way, by integrating $\sum_iw_i(\Reg^\omega(x^i,y^i)+\sum_t(y^i_t)^2)$ against $\mu$ and subtracting $\sum_iw_i\sum_t(y^i_t)^2$. Against a deterministic learner it is $\sum_iw_i\Reg(x^i,y^i)$.

For a play we write, as \citet[Section~2]{CQRZ} do,
\[
S_t=\sum_{i\le t}x_iy_i,\qquad V_t=\sum_{i\le t}x_i^2,\qquad R_t=\frac{S_t^2}{V_t}\quad(R_t=0\text{ if }V_t=0),
\]
and we put $\rho_t=S_t/\sqrt{V_t}$, so that $\rho_t^2=R_t$; further $s_t=x_t^2/V_t\in[0,1]$, and $\varepsilon_t=\sgn x_t$. The conventions for the empty and degenerate cases are $\rho_t=0$ and $s_t=0$ when $V_t=0$, $\varepsilon_t=1$ when $x_t=0$, and $\rho_0=S_0=V_0=0$. Finally $\clip(z)=\max(-1,\min(1,z))$. Since $|\clip(z)-y|\le|z-y|$ for every $y\in[-1,1]$, clipping a prediction to $[-1,1]$ never increases its loss when $|y|\le1$.

\subsection{Scaling and the regret identity}

\begin{lemma}[scaling]\label{lem:scaling}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Corollaries.regret_rescale_inv, RegretKappa.Lower.regret_rescale, RegretKappa.Upper.regret_learnerB, RegretKappa.Upper.bestLinearLoss_mul}
\mapnote{Lean proves the identity for every $B\ne0$.}
Let $B>0$, let $\mathcal L$ be a deterministic learner, and let $\mathcal L^B$ predict $\yh_t=B\,\mathcal L_t(x_1,\dots,x_t;y_1/B,\dots,y_{t-1}/B)$. For every play $(x,y)$, the regret of $\mathcal L^B$ on $(x,y)$ is $B^2$ times the regret of $\mathcal L$ on $(x,y/B)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Put $y'=y/B$ and let $\yh'_t$ be the predictions of $\mathcal L$ on $(x,y')$, so that $\mathcal L^B$ predicts $B\yh'_t$. Then $(B\yh'_t-y_t)^2=B^2(\yh'_t-y'_t)^2$ and $\sum_t(\theta x_t-y_t)^2=B^2\sum_t((\theta/B)x_t-y'_t)^2$; since $\theta\mapsto\theta/B$ is a bijection of $\R$, the infimum over $\theta$ is multiplied by $B^2$ as well.
\end{proof}

Since $(\mathcal L^{1/B})^B=\mathcal L$, every deterministic learner is of the form $\mathcal L^B$. The learner of Section~\ref{sec:upper} is described for $B=1$ and run for general $B$ in the form $\mathcal L^B$, which uses $B$; the adversary of Section~\ref{sec:lower} is described for $B=1$, and its outcomes are multiplied by $B$ for general $B$. The next identity is the completion of squares of \citet[Section~2.1]{CQRZ} in dimension one.

\begin{lemma}[regret identity]\label{lem:identity}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.bestLinearLoss_closed, RegretKappa.Upper.sum_sq_sub_bestLinearLoss, RegretKappa.Upper.regret_eq}
For every play, $\inf_{\theta\in\R}\sum_t(\theta x_t-y_t)^2=\sum_ty_t^2-R_T$. Hence
\[
\Reg(T)=\sum_{t=1}^T\bigl(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t\bigr)+\rho_T^2 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The sum equals $V_T\theta^2-2S_T\theta+\sum_ty_t^2$. If $V_T>0$, it is minimal at $\theta=S_T/V_T$, with value $\sum_ty_t^2-S_T^2/V_T$. If $V_T=0$, every $x_t$ vanishes, so $S_T=0$ and the sum is the constant $\sum_ty_t^2$. Subtracting from $\sum_t(\yh_t-y_t)^2=\sum_t(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)+\sum_ty_t^2$ gives the formula, since $\rho_T^2=R_T$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the state]\label{lem:state}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.rho_sq_le, RegretKappa.Upper.sq_sum_mul_le, RegretKappa.Upper.step}
\mapnote{In Lean the update (b) is the rewriting inside \lname{RegretKappa.Upper.step}, which also handles $V_{t-1}=0$ and $x_t=0$.}
For every play and every $t\ge1$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] if $|y_i|\le1$ for all $i\le t$, then $|\rho_t|\le\sqrt t$;
\item[(b)] $\rho_t=\rho_{t-1}\sqrt{1-s_t}+\varepsilon_ty_t\sqrt{s_t}$;
\item[(c)] if $\yh_t=\varepsilon_t\eta$ with $\eta\in\R$, then $\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t=\eta^2-2\eta\,\varepsilon_ty_t$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, $S_t^2\le(\sum_{i\le t}y_i^2)V_t\le tV_t$. (b) If $V_t=0$, then $x_t=0$ and $V_{t-1}=0$, so $\rho_t=\rho_{t-1}=0$ and $s_t=0$. If $V_t>0$, then $\rho_t=(S_{t-1}+x_ty_t)/\sqrt{V_t}$; the first part is $\rho_{t-1}\sqrt{V_{t-1}/V_t}=\rho_{t-1}\sqrt{1-s_t}$ (both sides vanish when $V_{t-1}=0$), and the second is $\varepsilon_ty_t\sqrt{s_t}$. (c) Use $\varepsilon_t^2=1$.
\end{proof}

With outcomes in $[-1,1]$, the game is therefore played on $\rho$. In round $t$ the adversary chooses $s=s_t\in[0,1]$, the learner, who knows $s$, chooses $\eta$ and predicts $\yh_t=\varepsilon_t\eta$, the adversary chooses $\tilde y=\varepsilon_ty_t\in[-1,1]$, the learner pays $\eta^2-2\eta\tilde y$, and $\rho$ moves from $\rho_{t-1}$ to $\rho_{t-1}\sqrt{1-s}+\tilde y\sqrt s$; at the end the learner pays $\rho_T^2$. When $V_{t-1}>0$ the adversary reaches every $s\in[0,1)$, with $x_t^2=V_{t-1}s/(1-s)$, and never $s=1$; when $V_{t-1}=0$ the state is $\rho_{t-1}=0$ and $s_t\in\{0,1\}$.

\section{An explicit learner}\label{sec:upper}

This section proves the upper bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The learner predicts with a clipped discrete derivative of the potential $\Phi_k(\rho)=2\log(C(\lambda_0+3k+\Gamma(\rho)))$, in which $\Gamma$ is a positive mixture of exponentials; Lemma~\ref{lem:potential} reduces its regret bound to an inequality for one round, Proposition~\ref{prop:P}, which the rest of the section proves.

For $|\theta|\ge1$ let $w(\theta)=e^{-\theta^2/2}\bigl(|\theta|^{-1}+2|\theta|^{-3}\bigr)$, and for $r\in\R$ let
\begin{equation}\label{eq:Gamma}
\Gamma(r)=\int_{|\theta|\ge1}e^{\theta r}w(\theta)\,d\theta=2\int_1^\infty\cosh(\theta r)\,e^{-\theta^2/2}\Bigl(\frac1\theta+\frac2{\theta^3}\Bigr)d\theta .
\end{equation}

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:upper}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.theoremU, RegretKappa.UpperSharp.theoremULog, RegretKappa.Corollaries.regret_rescale_learnerU_le}
\mapnote{Lean proves the first form for every $T\ge0$ and the second for $T\ge1$, for outcomes in $[-1,1]$ with the learner written out (\lname{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.learnerU}), and the case of $[-B,B]$ with the learner of Lemma~\ref{lem:scaling}.}
Let $T\ge1$ be an integer, $C=(\sqrt T+1)/\sqrt{2\pi}$ and $\lambda_0=e^2/C$. Let the learner predict, in round $t$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:learner}
\yh_t=\varepsilon_t\clip\Bigl(\frac12\log\frac{\lambda+\Gamma\bigl(\rho_{t-1}\sqrt{1-s_t}+\sqrt{s_t}\bigr)}{\lambda+\Gamma\bigl(\rho_{t-1}\sqrt{1-s_t}-\sqrt{s_t}\bigr)}\Bigr),\qquad\lambda=\lambda_0+3(T-t).
\end{equation}
Then on every play with outcomes in $[-1,1]$,
\begin{align*}
\Reg(T)\le u(T)&:=2\log\Bigl(e^2+\frac{(\sqrt T+1)(3T+2e^{-1/2})}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\Bigr)\\
&\le3\log T+2\log\frac{3}{\sqrt{2\pi}}+\frac{2}{\sqrt T}+\frac{0.81}{T}+\frac{12.35}{T^{3/2}}.
\end{align*}
For outcomes in $[-B,B]$, the learner obtained from \eqref{eq:learner} by Lemma~\ref{lem:scaling} has $\Reg(T)\le B^2u(T)$ on every play.
\end{theorem}

The bound $u(T)$ is the $U(T)$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The learner reads the feature $x_t$ only through its sign $\varepsilon_t$ and the step $s_t$, it predicts in $[-1,1]$, and it uses $T$ through $\lambda$, which counts the rounds left. Each prediction costs two evaluations of $\Gamma$, a one-dimensional integral. For instance $u(10^4)=28.010362\ldots$ and $u(10^6)=41.807879\ldots$ (Computation~\ref{comp:upper}).

\subsection{Reduction to one round}
For $0\le k\le T$ let $\Phi_k(\rho)=2\log\bigl(C(\lambda_0+3k+\Gamma(\rho))\bigr)$. For $\lambda>0$, $\rho\in\R$ and $s\in[0,1]$ let
\[
\eta(\lambda,\rho,s)=\clip\Bigl(\frac12\log\frac{\lambda+\Gamma(\rho c+\sigma)}{\lambda+\Gamma(\rho c-\sigma)}\Bigr),
\]
where here and below $c=\sqrt{1-s}$ and $\sigma=\sqrt s$. The learner \eqref{eq:learner} predicts $\yh_t=\varepsilon_t\,\eta(\lambda,\rho_{t-1},s_t)$ with $\lambda=\lambda_0+3(T-t)$.

\begin{proposition}[one round]\label{prop:P}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.step_abstract_sharp, RegretKappa.UpperSharp.step_paper}
\mapnote{Lean proves it in the normalization $\Gamma/2$ of the formalization, where the increment is $3/2$, and in the variables of the learner with increment $3$; the library Upper proves it with increment $120$ (\lname{RegretKappa.Upper.step_abstract}).}
For every $\lambda>0$, $\rho\in\R$, $s\in[0,1]$ and $y\in[-1,1]$, with $\eta=\eta(\lambda,\rho,s)$,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:P}
\eta^2-2\eta y+2\log\bigl(\lambda+\Gamma(\rho c+y\sigma)\bigr)\le2\log\bigl(\lambda+3+\Gamma(\rho)\bigr).
\end{equation}
\end{proposition}

The proof is in Section~\ref{subsec:proofU}, after the ingredients. Summed over the rounds, the proposition bounds the potential along the play.

\begin{lemma}[the potential]\label{lem:potential}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.potential_leU}
On every play with outcomes in $[-1,1]$, the learner \eqref{eq:learner} satisfies, for $0\le n\le T$,
\[
\sum_{t\le n}\bigl(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t\bigr)+\Phi_{T-n}(\rho_n)\le\Phi_T(0).
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By induction on $n$. For $n=0$ both sides are $\Phi_T(0)$, since $\rho_0=0$. Let $1\le t\le T$, $\lambda=\lambda_0+3(T-t)>0$, $s=s_t$ and $y=\varepsilon_ty_t\in[-1,1]$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:state}(b) and (c),
\[
\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t+\Phi_{T-t}(\rho_t)=\eta^2-2\eta y+2\log C+2\log\bigl(\lambda+\Gamma(\rho_{t-1}c+y\sigma)\bigr)
\]
with $\eta=\eta(\lambda,\rho_{t-1},s)$, and Proposition~\ref{prop:P} bounds the right side by $2\log C+2\log(\lambda+3+\Gamma(\rho_{t-1}))=\Phi_{T-t+1}(\rho_{t-1})$. Adding $\sum_{i<t}(\yh_i^2-2\yh_iy_i)$ and the induction hypothesis at $n=t-1$ gives the claim at $n=t$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The potential}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:Gamma}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Gam_neg,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Gam_nonneg,
  RegretKappa.Upper.G_mono,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Gam_zero,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.mom_odd,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Mw_one,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Mw_add_two,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Mw_eq_GamC,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Dg_hasSum,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Dg_nonneg,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.Dg_mono,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.coeff_le,
  RegretKappa.UpperSharp.G_sqrt_sub_le}
\mapnote{Lean proves (c) and (d) in the normalization $\Gamma/2$, with $\delta/2$; it writes $K_m=e^{-1/2}k_m$ with integers $k_1=1$, $k_{m+1}=1+2mk_m$.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] $\Gamma$ is even and nonnegative, $\Gamma(r)\le\Gamma(r')$ whenever $|r|\le|r'|$, and $\Gamma(0)=2e^{-1/2}$.
\item[(b)] For $n\ge0$ let $\Gamma_n=2\int_1^\infty\theta^{2n}e^{-\theta^2/2}(\theta^{-1}+2\theta^{-3})\,d\theta$, and for $m\ge0$ let $K_m=\int_1^\infty\theta^{2m-1}e^{-\theta^2/2}\,d\theta$. Then $K_1=e^{-1/2}$, $K_{m+1}=e^{-1/2}+2mK_m$ for $m\ge1$, and $\Gamma_n=2(K_n+2K_{n-1})$ for $n\ge1$.
\item[(c)] For $x>0$ let $\delta(x)=\int_1^\infty\theta\sinh(\theta\sqrt x)\,x^{-1/2}\,e^{-\theta^2/2}(\theta^{-1}+2\theta^{-3})\,d\theta$. Then $\delta(x)=\sum_{n\ge1}n\Gamma_nx^{n-1}/(2n)!$, the function $\delta$ is nonnegative and nondecreasing on $(0,\infty)$, and $n\Gamma_n/(2n)!\le2/n!$ for $n\ge1$.
\item[(d)] For $0\le X\le Y$ with $Y>0$, $\Gamma(\sqrt Y)-\Gamma(\sqrt X)\le(Y-X)\,\delta(Y)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(a) The weight $w$ is even, positive and has Gaussian decay, and $\cosh(\theta r)$ is even in $r$ and nondecreasing in $|r|$, so $\Gamma$, written with $\cosh$ as in \eqref{eq:Gamma}, is finite, even, nonnegative and nondecreasing in $|r|$. Since $\frac{d}{d\theta}\bigl(-\theta^{-2}e^{-\theta^2/2}\bigr)=e^{-\theta^2/2}(\theta^{-1}+2\theta^{-3})$, $\Gamma(0)=2e^{-1/2}$.

(b) Expanding the bracket gives $\Gamma_n=2(K_n+2K_{n-1})$ for $n\ge1$. Next $K_1=\int_1^\infty\theta e^{-\theta^2/2}d\theta=e^{-1/2}$, and an integration by parts of $\theta^{2m}\cdot\theta e^{-\theta^2/2}$ gives $K_{m+1}=e^{-1/2}+2m\int_1^\infty\theta^{2m-1}e^{-\theta^2/2}d\theta=e^{-1/2}+2mK_m$.

(c) For $\theta\ge1$, $\theta\sinh(\theta\sqrt x)/\sqrt x=\sum_{n\ge1}2n\,\theta^{2n}x^{n-1}/(2n)!$, with nonnegative terms; integrating term by term against $e^{-\theta^2/2}(\theta^{-1}+2\theta^{-3})$ gives the series, whose coefficients are nonnegative, so $\delta$ is nonnegative and nondecreasing. For the bound, the substitution $v=\theta^2/2$ gives $K_m\le\int_0^\infty\theta^{2m-1}e^{-\theta^2/2}d\theta=2^{m-1}(m-1)!$ for $m\ge1$. For $n\ge2$ this gives $\Gamma_n\le2\bigl(2^{n-1}(n-1)!+2^{n-1}(n-2)!\bigr)\le2^{n+1}(n-1)!$, and $\Gamma_1=2e^{-1/2}+4K_0\le4$, since $K_0\le0.28$ (Computation~\ref{comp:upper}). Since $(2n)!=\binom{2n}{n}(n!)^2\ge2^n(n!)^2$, we get $n\Gamma_n/(2n)!\le n2^{n+1}(n-1)!/(2^n(n!)^2)=2/n!$.

(d) For $\theta\ge1$ the function $x\mapsto\cosh(\theta\sqrt x)=\sum_n\theta^{2n}x^n/(2n)!$ is convex on $[0,\infty)$, with derivative $\theta\sinh(\theta\sqrt x)/(2\sqrt x)$ at $x>0$, so
\[
\cosh(\theta\sqrt Y)-\cosh(\theta\sqrt X)\le(Y-X)\,\frac{\theta\sinh(\theta\sqrt Y)}{2\sqrt Y}.
\] Multiplying by $2e^{-\theta^2/2}(\theta^{-1}+2\theta^{-3})$ and integrating over $\theta\ge1$ gives the claim.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Two outcomes}

For $y\in[-1,1]$ write $A(y)=\lambda+\Gamma(\rho c+y\sigma)$ and $A_\pm=A(\pm1)$. The left side of \eqref{eq:P} is $2\log\bigl(e^{\eta^2/2-\eta y}A(y)\bigr)$.

\begin{lemma}[endpoints]\label{lem:endpoints}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.G_convex, RegretKappa.Upper.exp_mul_le}
\mapnote{Lean proves the inequality for $\Gamma/2$ in place of $\Gamma$ and for every $\rho c$ and $\sigma$ in $\R$, and for the term $\lambda e^{-\eta y}$ separately.}
For $\eta\in\R$, $\lambda\ge0$, $\rho\in\R$, $s\in[0,1]$ and $y\in[-1,1]$,
\[
e^{-\eta y}A(y)\le\frac{1+y}2\,e^{-\eta}A_++\frac{1-y}2\,e^{\eta}A_- .
\]
In particular the left side of \eqref{eq:P} is at most the larger of its values at $y=1$ and $y=-1$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By \eqref{eq:Gamma}, $e^{-\eta y}A(y)=\lambda e^{-\eta y}+\int_{|\theta|\ge1}e^{\theta\rho c}\,e^{y(\theta\sigma-\eta)}\,w(\theta)\,d\theta$. For fixed $\theta$ the functions $y\mapsto e^{-\eta y}$ and $y\mapsto e^{y(\theta\sigma-\eta)}$ are convex, so on $[-1,1]$ each lies below its chord; integrating against the positive weight gives the inequality. Its right side is a convex combination of the values of $e^{-\eta y}A(y)$ at $y=\pm1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Mixability}

\begin{lemma}[two points]\label{lem:mix}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.two_point, RegretKappa.Upper.two_point_mgf}
\mapnote{Lean derives it from Hoeffding's lemma in Mathlib, applied to the law on two points of \lname{RegretKappa.Upper.two_point_mgf}.}
For $\eta\in[-1,1]$ and $z\in\R$,
\[
(1+\eta)\,e^{z-\eta}+(1-\eta)\,e^{-(z-\eta)}\le2\,e^{(z^2-\eta^2)/2}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $L(t)=\log\bigl(((1+\eta)e^t+(1-\eta)e^{-t})/2\bigr)$, the logarithm of the moment generating function of the law on $\{-1,1\}$ with mean $\eta$. Then $L(0)=0$, $L'(0)=\eta$, and $L''=1-(L')^2\le1$, since $L'(t)=((1+\eta)e^t-(1-\eta)e^{-t})/((1+\eta)e^t+(1-\eta)e^{-t})$. By Taylor's formula $L(t)\le\eta t+t^2/2$, which is Hoeffding's lemma for this law. At $t=z-\eta$, $\eta t+t^2/2=(z^2-\eta^2)/2$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the prediction]\label{lem:choice}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.mix_alg}
Let $p,q>0$ and $Z\in\R$ satisfy $(1+\eta)e^{-\eta}p+(1-\eta)e^{\eta}q\le2e^{-\eta^2/2}Z$ for every $\eta\in[-1,1]$. Then $\eta^*=\clip\bigl(\frac12\log(p/q)\bigr)$ satisfies
\[
e^{-\eta^*}p\le e^{-\eta^{*2}/2}Z\qquad\text{and}\qquad e^{\eta^*}q\le e^{-\eta^{*2}/2}Z .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $r=\frac12\log(p/q)$. If $|r|\le1$, then $\eta^*=r$ and $e^{-r}p=e^{r}q$, so the hypothesis at $\eta=r$ reads $2e^{r}q\le2e^{-r^2/2}Z$, which is both inequalities. If $r>1$, then $\eta^*=1$, the hypothesis at $\eta=1$ reads $2e^{-1}p\le2e^{-1/2}Z$, and $e\,q<e^{-1}p$ since $p>e^2q$. The case $r<-1$ is symmetric.
\end{proof}

Let $\nu$ be a nonzero finite positive measure on $\R$ with $I_\nu=\int e^{z^2/2}\,\nu(dz)<\infty$. Integrating Lemma~\ref{lem:mix} against $\nu$ and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:choice} with $p=\int e^{z}\,d\nu$, $q=\int e^{-z}\,d\nu$ and $Z=I_\nu$ shows that $\eta^*$ satisfies $e^{(\eta^{*2}-2\eta^*y)/2}\int e^{yz}\,\nu(dz)\le I_\nu$ for $y=1$ and $y=-1$. This is a form of the mixability of the square loss at rate $1/2$ for the outcomes $\pm1$ \citep[Section~2.4, Lemma~2]{Vovk01}; for outcomes in $\{0,1\}$ it is the mixability at rate $2$ of \citet[Section~2, Example~4]{Vovk98} and \citet[Example~3.13]{HKW}, after the change of outcome $\omega\mapsto2\omega-1$. The prediction of Proposition~\ref{prop:P} is the $\eta^*$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:choice} for $p=A_+$ and $q=A_-$, and the next corollary takes two choices of $Z$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:mix}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.G_tangent, RegretKappa.Upper.mix_small, RegretKappa.Upper.mix_large}
\mapnote{Lean proves (a) and (b) in the normalization $\Gamma/2$.}
Let $\lambda>0$, $\rho\in\R$, $s\in[0,1]$, $y\in[-1,1]$, and $\eta=\eta(\lambda,\rho,s)$.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] If $s<1$, the left side of \eqref{eq:P} is at most $2\log(\lambda+\Ghat(\rho,s))$, where
\[
\Ghat(\rho,s)=\int_{|\theta|\ge1}e^{\theta^2s/2}\,e^{\theta c\rho}\,w(\theta)\,d\theta<\infty .
\]
\item[(b)] The left side of \eqref{eq:P} is at most $2\log\bigl(\lambda+\max(\Gamma(\rho c+\sigma),\Gamma(\rho c-\sigma))\bigr)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The numbers $A_\pm$ are positive, since $\lambda>0$ and $\Gamma\ge0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(a)). By Lemma~\ref{lem:endpoints} it suffices to take $y=\pm1$, and by Lemma~\ref{lem:choice} with $p=A_+$ and $q=A_-$, the left side of \eqref{eq:P} at $y=\pm1$ is at most $2\log Z$ for every $Z$ that satisfies its hypothesis. (a) Since $s<1$, $e^{\theta^2s/2}w(\theta)$ has Gaussian decay and $\Ghat(\rho,s)$ is finite. Multiply Lemma~\ref{lem:mix} at $z=\theta\sigma$ by $e^{\theta c\rho}w(\theta)$, integrate over $|\theta|\ge1$, and add $\lambda$ times Lemma~\ref{lem:mix} at $z=0$; this is the hypothesis with $Z=\lambda+\Ghat(\rho,s)$. (b) Lemma~\ref{lem:mix} at $z=0$ gives $(1+\eta)e^{-\eta}+(1-\eta)e^{\eta}\le2e^{-\eta^2/2}$, and $1\pm\eta\ge0$; multiplied by $Z=\max(A_+,A_-)$ this gives the hypothesis.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Small steps}
The weight $|\theta|^{-1}$ makes $\Gamma$ nearly invariant under the scaling that turns $\Ghat$ back into $\Gamma$: after the substitution $u=c\theta$, the factor $e^{\theta^2s/2-\theta^2/2}$ becomes $e^{-u^2/2}$ and $d\theta/|\theta|=du/|u|$. Only the cutoff at $|\theta|=1$ and the factor $1+2/\theta^2$ change.

\begin{lemma}[the substitution]\label{lem:excess}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Upper.Ghat_subst}
\mapnote{Lean proves it in the normalization $\Gamma/2$.}
For $s\in[0,1)$ and $\rho\in\R$,
\[
\Ghat(\rho,s)=2\int_c^\infty\cosh(u\rho)\,e^{-u^2/2}\Bigl(\frac1u+\frac{2c^2}{u^3}\Bigr)du .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $\theta^2s/2-\theta^2/2=-\theta^2c^2/2$, $\Ghat(\rho,s)=2\int_1^\infty\cosh(\theta c\rho)\,e^{-\theta^2c^2/2}(\theta^{-1}+2\theta^{-3})\,d\theta$. As $s<1$, $c>0$, and the substitution $u=c\theta$ turns $d\theta/\theta$ into $du/u$ and $d\theta/\theta^3$ into $c^2du/u^3$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[small steps]\label{lem:small}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.small_step_sharp, RegretKappa.UpperSharp.quartic_le}
\mapnote{Lean proves it in the normalization $\Gamma/2$ ($2.8857/2$), with the maximum of $f$ bounded by $4.32855$ from rational coefficients; the library Upper proves $\Ghat-\Gamma\le24$ for $s\le3/4$ (\lname{RegretKappa.Upper.small_step}).}
For $s\in[0,2/3]$ and $\rho\in\R$, $\Ghat(\rho,s)-\Gamma(\rho)\le2.8857$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
For $s=0$ both sides of $\Ghat=\Gamma$ agree; let $0<s\le2/3$, so that $c^2\in[1/3,1)$. Split the integral of Lemma~\ref{lem:excess} at $u=1$ and subtract $\Gamma(\rho)=2\int_1^\infty\cosh(u\rho)e^{-u^2/2}(u^{-1}+2u^{-3})\,du$; since $2c^2-2=-2s$,
\begin{gather*}
\Ghat(\rho,s)-\Gamma(\rho)=D(\rho,s)-4sH(\rho),\\
D(\rho,s)=2\int_c^1\cosh(u\rho)\,e^{-u^2/2}\Bigl(\frac1u+\frac{2c^2}{u^3}\Bigr)du,\qquad H(\rho)=\int_1^\infty\frac{\cosh(u\rho)\,e^{-u^2/2}}{u^3}\,du.
\end{gather*}
Expanding $\cosh$,
\begin{gather*}
\Ghat(\rho,s)-\Gamma(\rho)=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{\rho^{2n}}{(2n)!}\bigl(D_n(s)-4sH_n\bigr),\\
D_n(s)=2\int_c^1u^{2n}e^{-u^2/2}\Bigl(\frac1u+\frac{2c^2}{u^3}\Bigr)du,\qquad H_n=\int_1^\infty u^{2n-3}e^{-u^2/2}\,du .
\end{gather*}
On $[c,1]$ we have $e^{-u^2/2}\le e^{-c^2/2}$. The function $s\mapsto-\log(1-s)$ is convex and $\ell_0=\frac32\log3$ is its chord slope on $[0,2/3]$, so $\log(1/c)\le\ell_0s/2$; and $1-c^{2m}=1-(1-s)^m\le ms$ for $m\ge1$. Hence:
\begin{itemize}
\item $D_0\le2e^{-c^2/2}\bigl(\log(1/c)+1-c^2\bigr)\le2e^{-1/6}(\ell_0/2+1)\,s$;
\item $D_1\le2e^{-c^2/2}\bigl((1-c^2)/2+2c^2\log(1/c)\bigr)\le2s\,e^{-x/2}(1/2+\ell_0x)$ with $x=c^2\le1$, and this function of $x$ has derivative $e^{-x/2}(\ell_0-1/4-\ell_0x/2)>0$ on $[0,1]$, so $D_1\le2e^{-1/2}(1/2+\ell_0)\,s$;
\item for $n\ge2$, $D_n\le2e^{-c^2/2}\bigl(\frac{1-c^{2n}}{2n}+2c^2\frac{1-c^{2n-2}}{2n-2}\bigr)\le2s\,e^{-x/2}(1/2+x)\le3e^{-1/2}s$, since $e^{-x/2}(1/2+x)$ has derivative $e^{-x/2}(3/4-x/2)>0$ on $[0,1]$.
\end{itemize}
Let $f(x)=\sum_{n=0}^4f_nx^n/(2n)!$ with
\[
f_0=2e^{-1/6}\Bigl(1+\frac{\ell_0}2\Bigr)-4H_0,\quad f_1=2e^{-1/2}\Bigl(\frac12+\ell_0\Bigr)-4H_1,\quad f_n=3e^{-1/2}-4H_n\ (n=2,3,4).
\]
So $(D_n(s)-4sH_n)/s\le f_n$ for $n\le4$, and for $n\ge5$ it is at most $3e^{-1/2}-4H_n\le0$, because $H_n=K_{n-1}$ increases with $n$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(b) and $H_2=e^{-1/2}$. Dropping the terms $n\ge5$ gives $\Ghat-\Gamma\le s\,f(\rho^2)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(b), $H_1=K_0$, $H_2=e^{-1/2}$, $H_3=3e^{-1/2}$, $H_4=13e^{-1/2}$, and an integration by parts gives $H_0=(e^{-1/2}-K_0)/2$; so $f_2=-e^{-1/2}$, $f_3=-9e^{-1/2}$, $f_4=-49e^{-1/2}$. These are negative, so $f$ is concave on $[0,\infty)$, and $f'(0)=f_1/2>0$; its maximum is at the unique positive root $x^*=3.9683254\ldots$ of the cubic $f'$, where $f(x^*)=4.3285373\ldots$, below $4.32855$ (Computation~\ref{comp:upper}). Hence $\Ghat-\Gamma\le s\,f(\rho^2)\le\frac23f(x^*)=2.8856915\ldots$. This is \lname{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.small_step_sharp} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Large steps}

\begin{lemma}[large steps]\label{lem:large}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.large_step_sharp, RegretKappa.UpperSharp.psi_le, RegretKappa.UpperSharp.piece_bound}
\mapnote{Lean proves it in the normalization $\Gamma/2$ ($2.7053/2$), with one theorem for each piece (\lname{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.piece_1} to \lname{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.piece_22}); the library Upper proves $\Gamma(\rho c\pm\sigma)\le\Gamma(\rho)+\Gamma(2)$ for $s\ge3/4$, with $\Gamma(2)\le120$ (\lname{RegretKappa.Upper.large_step}).}
\codemap{code/lean-upper-sharp/pieces_lean.gp > code/lean-upper-sharp/out/pieces_lean.txt, code/lean-upper-sharp/pieces.gp > code/lean-upper-sharp/out/pieces.txt}
For $s\in[2/3,1]$ and $\rho\in\R$, $\max\bigl(\Gamma(\rho c+\sigma),\Gamma(\rho c-\sigma)\bigr)\le\Gamma(\rho)+2.7053$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
If $\rho\ge0$, then $|\rho c-\sigma|\le\rho c+\sigma$; if $\rho<0$, then $\Gamma(\rho c\pm\sigma)=\Gamma(|\rho|c\mp\sigma)$. Since $\Gamma$ is even and nondecreasing in $|r|$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(a)), the maximum is $\Gamma(|\rho|c+\sigma)$ in both cases, and it suffices to show $\Gamma(\rho c+\sigma)\le\Gamma(\rho)+2.7053$ for $\rho\ge0$. Let $\rho\ge0$ and $\rho_*=1/\sqrt2$.

(i) The function $s\mapsto\rho\sqrt{1-s}+\sqrt s$ is concave on $[0,1]$, with maximum $\sqrt{1+\rho^2}$ at $s=1/(1+\rho^2)$. So on $[2/3,1]$ it is at most $m(\rho)=\sqrt{1+\rho^2}$ if $\rho\le\rho_*$ (then $1/(1+\rho^2)\ge2/3$), and at most its value at $s=2/3$, $m(\rho)=\rho/\sqrt3+\sqrt{2/3}$, if $\rho\ge\rho_*$. Both expressions equal $\sqrt{3/2}$ at $\rho_*$, and $m$ is nondecreasing. Since $\Gamma$ is nondecreasing in $|r|$, it suffices to bound $\psi(\rho)=\Gamma(m(\rho))-\Gamma(\rho)$ by $2.7053$.

(ii) Let $q(\rho)=m(\rho)^2-\rho^2$. Then $q=1$ on $[0,\rho_*]$, and $q(\rho)=-\frac23\rho^2+\frac{2\sqrt2}3\rho+\frac23$ on $[\rho_*,\infty)$, a concave quadratic with vertex at $\rho_*$; so $q$ is nonincreasing on $[0,\infty)$. If $q(\rho)\le0$, then $m(\rho)\le\rho$ and $\psi(\rho)\le0$; this holds for $\rho\ge2$, since $q$ vanishes at $(1+\sqrt3)/\sqrt2<2$.

(iii) If $q(\rho)>0$, Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(d) with $X=\rho^2$ and $Y=m(\rho)^2$ gives $\psi(\rho)\le q(\rho)\,\delta(m(\rho)^2)$. If $\rho$ lies in a piece $[\rho_{i-1},\rho_i]$, this is at most $q_i\,\delta(x_i)$ for all numbers $q_i\ge q(\rho_{i-1})$ and $x_i\ge m(\rho_i)^2$, since $q$ is nonincreasing, $m$ and $\delta$ are nondecreasing, and $\delta\ge0$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(c)).

(iv) Take $\rho_i=i/11$ for $i=0,\dots,22$, so that the pieces cover $[0,2]$. For $0<x<32$, Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(c) and the geometric series give
\[

\]
with $\Gamma_n$ from Lemma~\ref{lem:Gamma}(b). With $q_i$ and $x_i$ the upward roundings to six decimals of $q(\rho_{i-1})$ and $m(\rho_i)^2$, as in the formal proof, all $x_i$ below $4$, the twenty-two products of $q_i$ and this bound at $x_i$, with the $\Gamma_n$ computed from the rational upper bounds of $K_0$ and $e^{-1/2}$ of Computation~\ref{comp:upper}, which can only increase them, are at most $2.7053$; the largest, $2.6942861\ldots$, is on the piece $[13/11,14/11]$. This is \lname{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.large_step_sharp} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\subsection{The terminal condition}

\begin{lemma}[terminal condition]\label{lem:terminal}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.terminal_ineq, RegretKappa.UpperSharp.terminal_sharp}
\mapnote{The library Upper proves the consequence with $C=2(\sqrt T+1)$ and $\lambda_0=2/C$, from a lower bound of $\Gamma$ on the window $\theta\in(r,r+1]$ (\lname{RegretKappa.Upper.terminal}).}
\codemap{code/upper-bound-certificate/c4_lemma71.sage > code/upper-bound-certificate/out/c4_lemma71.txt}
For $\rho\ge2$, $\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{\rho^2/2}\le(\rho+1)\Gamma(\rho)$. Consequently $C(\lambda_0+\Gamma(\rho))\ge e^{\rho^2/2}$ for $|\rho|\le\sqrt T$, that is $\Phi_0(\rho)\ge\rho^2$ there.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $\phi(x)=e^{-x^2/2}/\sqrt{2\pi}$, let $N$ be the standard normal distribution function and $\bar N=1-N$. Put $I(\rho)=e^{-\rho^2/2}\Gamma(\rho)$ and $\gamma(\theta)=e^{-(\theta-\rho)^2/2}$. Then
\[
I(\rho)=\int_1^\infty\gamma(\theta)\bigl(1+e^{-2\theta\rho}\bigr)\Bigl(\frac1\theta+\frac2{\theta^3}\Bigr)d\theta\ge\int_1^\infty\frac{\gamma(\theta)}\theta\,d\theta .
\]
Let $M_0=\int_1^\infty\gamma=\sqrt{2\pi}\,N(\rho-1)$ and $M_1=\int_1^\infty\theta\gamma=\rho M_0+e^{-(\rho-1)^2/2}$, and $a=M_0/M_1$. For $\theta>0$, $1/\theta\ge2a-a^2\theta$, since $\theta(1/\theta-2a+a^2\theta)=(1-a\theta)^2$; multiplying by $\gamma$ and integrating over $\theta\ge1$ gives $\int_1^\infty\frac\gamma\theta\,d\theta\ge2aM_0-a^2M_1=M_0^2/M_1$. With $x=\rho-1$ this gives
\[
\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}}{I(\rho)}\le\frac{\sqrt{2\pi}\,M_1}{M_0^2}=\frac{\rho}{N(x)}+\frac{\phi(x)}{N(x)^2}.
\]
For $\rho\ge2$, $x\ge1$, and $\rho/N(x)-\rho=\rho\bar N(x)/N(x)\le\frac{\rho}{\rho-1}\frac{\phi(x)}{N(x)}\le\frac{2\phi(1)}{N(1)}$, by the Mills ratio bound $\bar N(x)\le\phi(x)/x$, by $\rho/(\rho-1)\le2$, and because $\phi$ decreases and $N$ increases on $[1,\infty)$; for the same reason $\phi(x)/N(x)^2\le\phi(1)/N(1)^2$. Since $2\phi(1)/N(1)+\phi(1)/N(1)^2=0.91703362\ldots<1$ (Computation~\ref{comp:upper}), we get $\sqrt{2\pi}/I(\rho)\le\rho+1$, which is the inequality. For the consequence: if $|\rho|\le2$, then $C\lambda_0=e^2\ge e^{\rho^2/2}$; if $2\le|\rho|\le\sqrt T$, then $C\,\Gamma(\rho)\ge(|\rho|+1)\Gamma(\rho)/\sqrt{2\pi}\ge e^{\rho^2/2}$ by evenness. This is \lname{RegretKappa.UpperSharp.terminal_sharp} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\section{An explicit adversary}\label{sec:lower}

This section proves the lower bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}. The adversary plays in two phases. In the first, of $\lfloor T/2\rfloor$ rounds, it drives $R_t=\rho_t^2$ up to a level $L$ close to $2\log T$ with fair random signs as outcomes, against which no prediction gains anything, and then holds the state. In the second it draws a parameter from a prior centred at the least squares estimate of the first phase and generates the outcomes from it; estimating this parameter costs every learner about $\log T$ more. Until the last part of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}, $B=1$; the outcomes of the adversary lie in $\{-1,0,1\}$.

\subsection{The statement}
Let $j_0$, $L$, $k_0$, $c_1$ and $b(T)$ be as in \eqref{eq:bT}, and put
\[
a=\sqrt L,\qquad a_+=a+\sqrt{5/8}\,e^{-L/4},\qquad T_1=\lfloor T/2\rfloor,\qquad J=\frac{\pi^2}4,
\]
so that $k_0=T-T_1$.

\begin{theorem}\label{thm:lower}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.lowerSharpAdv, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.lowerSharpSimplified, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.lowerSharpBound}
\mapnote{(a), (b) and (c) are \lname{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.LowerSharpAdv}, \lname{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.LowerSharpSimplified} and \lname{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.LowerSharpBound}; the proof of (a) builds the plays of $\mathcal A_T$ (\lname{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.adv_core}).}
Let $T\ge T_0=355713$ be an integer.
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(a)] There is an adversary that ignores the predictions and plays finitely many plays $(x^i,y^i)$, with probabilities $w_i$, with features $x^i_t\ge0$ and outcomes $y^i_t\in\{-1,0,1\}$, against which every deterministic learner has $\sum_iw_i\Reg(x^i,y^i)\ge b(T)$.
\item[(b)] $b(T)\ge3\log T-\log\log T-2\log(\log\log T+2.1)-14.6\ge3\log T-2\log\log T-15.2$.
\item[(c)] For every $B>0$, every deterministic learner has a play with outcomes in $[-B,B]$ on which $\Reg(T)\ge B^2b(T)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

The proof shows that the adversary $\mathcal A_T$ of Section~\ref{subsec:adversary} is one as in (a). For instance $b(T_0)=19.670890\ldots$, $b(10^6)=22.629622\ldots$ and $b(10^{10})=49.386016\ldots$. The ratio $b(T)/\log T$ tends to $3$ slowly: to six decimals it is $1.637987$ at $T=10^6$ and $2.901018$ at $T=10^{100}$.

\subsection{The adversary}\label{subsec:adversary}
The adversary $\mathcal A_T$ uses independent random signs $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_{T_1}$, uniform on $\{-1,1\}$, a real random variable $Z$, independent of the signs, with density
\[
f_Z(z)=\tfrac12\cos^2(\pi z/4)\quad\text{on }[-2,2],
\]
and further independent coin flips for the outcomes of its second phase.

\emph{The move rule.} Let $s\colon\R\to(0,1)$ be the even function equal to $s_j$ on $\{|\rho|\in[j/10,(j+1)/10)\}$ for $j=0,\dots,27$, and to $\frac58e^{-\rho^2/2}$ for $|\rho|\ge2.8$, where $10^4(s_0,\dots,s_{27})$ is
\[
\begin{gathered}
(6242,6180,6058,5879,5648,5373,5060,4718,4355,3980,3601,3226,2861,2513,\\
2184,1880,1602,1352,1129,934,764,620,497,395,311,242,187,142),
\end{gathered}
\]
the values of $\frac58e^{-m^2/2}$ at the midpoints $m=j/10+1/20$, rounded to four decimals. The stopped move is $s_L(\rho)=s(\rho)$ if $\rho^2<L$, and $s_L(\rho)=0$ if $\rho^2\ge L$.

\emph{Phase 1, rounds $1$ to $T_1$.} Round $1$ is $x_1=1$, $y_1=\xi_1$. For $1\le t<T_1$, round $t+1$ is
\[
x_{t+1}=\sqrt{V_t\,s_L(\rho_t)/(1-s_L(\rho_t))},\qquad y_{t+1}=\xi_{t+1}.
\]
Once $\rho_t^2\ge L$ the features are $0$, so $\rho$ and $V$ keep their values. Let $\Succ=\{\rho_{T_1}^2\ge L\}$ be the event that phase 1 succeeds.

\emph{Phase 2, rounds $T_1+1$ to $T$, on $\Succ$.} Let $k=k_0$, $r=|\rho_{T_1}|+2$ and $\theta^*=(\rho_{T_1}+Z)/\sqrt{V_{T_1}}$, that is $S_{T_1}/V_{T_1}+Z/\sqrt{V_{T_1}}$. For $i=1,\dots,k$, round $T_1+i$ is
\[
x_{T_1+i}=\alpha_i\sqrt{V_{T_1}},\quad\alpha_i=\frac1{\sqrt2\,r}\sqrt{\frac ik},\qquad y_{T_1+i}=\begin{cases}1&\text{with probability }(1+\theta^*x_{T_1+i})/2,\\-1&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}
\]
the outcomes being independent given $\theta^*$ and the past. Since $\theta^*x_{T_1+i}=(\rho_{T_1}+Z)\alpha_i$ and $|Z|\le2$, we have $|\theta^*x_{T_1+i}|\le\nu_i:=\sqrt{i/(2k)}\le1/\sqrt2$, so these are probabilities, and $y_{T_1+i}$ has conditional mean $\theta^*x_{T_1+i}$.

\emph{Failure, off $\Succ$}: $x_t=y_t=0$ for $t>T_1$.

The adversary never looks at the predictions. Its phase-1 features are functions of the earlier signs and its phase-2 features of $(\rho_{T_1},V_{T_1})$, so they carry no information on $Z$; and since the signs and the phase-2 outcomes take finitely many values, $\mathcal A_T$ plays finitely many plays, each with its probability.

\begin{lemma}[the chain]\label{lem:chain}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.sum_xS_sq, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.sum_xS_mul, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.rhoS_eq_chainOf}
For $1\le t<T_1$, $V_{t+1}=V_t/(1-s_L(\rho_t))$ and $\rho_{t+1}=\rho_t\sqrt{1-s_L(\rho_t)}+\xi_{t+1}\sqrt{s_L(\rho_t)}$, and $S_t=\rho_t\sqrt{V_t}$ with $V_t\ge1$ for $1\le t\le T_1$. So $(\rho_t)_{1\le t\le T_1}$ is the Markov chain on $\R$ started at $\rho_1=\xi_1$ that moves from $\rho$ to $\rho\sqrt{1-s_L(\rho)}+\sqrt{s_L(\rho)}$ or to $\rho\sqrt{1-s_L(\rho)}-\sqrt{s_L(\rho)}$, with probability $1/2$ each; we call it the stopped chain, since it stays at $\rho$ once $\rho^2\ge L$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By the definition of $x_{t+1}$, $V_{t+1}=V_t+V_ts_L(\rho_t)/(1-s_L(\rho_t))=V_t/(1-s_L(\rho_t))$, so $x_{t+1}^2/V_{t+1}=s_L(\rho_t)$, and $x_{t+1}\ge0$. Lemma~\ref{lem:state}(b) gives the update of $\rho$, and $y_{t+1}=\xi_{t+1}$ is a fair sign independent of the past. Also $V_1=1$ and $V_t$ does not decrease.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase 1: a hitting time}

Let $U(\rho)=8e^{\rho^2/2}$.

\begin{lemma}[drift]\label{lem:drift}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.drift, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.driftGap_large, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.drift_table}
\codemap{code/lean-upper-sharp/lsharp_margins.gp > code/lean-upper-sharp/out/lsharp_margins.txt}
For every $\rho\in\R$, with $s=s(\rho)$,
\[
\tfrac12\Bigl[U\bigl(\rho\sqrt{1-s}+\sqrt s\bigr)+U\bigl(\rho\sqrt{1-s}-\sqrt s\bigr)\Bigr]\ge U(\rho)+1 .
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Since $(\rho\sqrt{1-s}\pm\sqrt s)^2=\rho^2(1-s)+s\pm2\rho\sqrt{s(1-s)}$, the left side is $8h_s(\rho)$ with
\[
h_s(\rho)=e^{(\rho^2(1-s)+s)/2}\cosh\bigl(\rho\sqrt{s(1-s)}\bigr).
\]
Both sides are even in $\rho$, so let $\rho\ge0$.

(a) Let $\rho\ge2.8$, so $s=\frac58e^{-\rho^2/2}$. Write $h_s(\rho)=e^{\rho^2/2}e^{-z}\cosh x$ with $z=s(\rho^2-1)/2\ge0$ and $x^2=\rho^2s(1-s)=2c'$. From $\cosh x\ge1+c'$ and $e^{-z}\ge1-z\ge0$, as $z\le\frac5{16}\rho^2e^{-\rho^2/2}<1$, we get $e^{-z}\cosh x\ge(1-z)(1+c')$, and $(1-z)(1+c')-1=c'-z-zc'$ with $c'-z=s/2-\rho^2s^2/2$ and $zc'=s^2\rho^2(\rho^2-1)(1-s)/4\le s^2\rho^4/4$. Hence
\[
8h_s(\rho)-U(\rho)\ge8e^{\rho^2/2}s\Bigl(\frac12-s\Bigl(\frac{\rho^2}2+\frac{\rho^4}4\Bigr)\Bigr)=\frac52-\frac{25}8\chi(\rho),\qquad\chi(\rho)=e^{-\rho^2/2}\Bigl(\frac{\rho^2}2+\frac{\rho^4}4\Bigr).
\]
As a function of $v=\rho^2$, $e^{-v/2}(v/2+v^2/4)$ has derivative $e^{-v/2}(1/2+v/4-v^2/8)$, negative for $v>1+\sqrt5$; since $2.8^2=7.84$, $\chi$ is decreasing on $[2.8,\infty)$, and $\chi(\rho)\le\chi(2.8)=0.38266328\ldots<12/25$. So $8h_s(\rho)-U(\rho)\ge\frac52-\frac{25}8\cdot\frac{12}{25}=1$.

(b) Let $0\le\rho<2.8$. On a cell $[j/10,(j+1)/10)$ the step $s=s_j$ is a constant rational number in $(0,1)$, and $h_s$ is nondecreasing on $[0,\infty)$, both of its factors being so. Hence on a subinterval $[\ell,\ell']$ of the cell, $8h_s(\rho)-U(\rho)\ge8h_s(\ell)-8e^{\ell'^2/2}$. For $N\ge1$ let
\[
e_N(q)=\sum_{n\le N}\frac{q^n}{n!},\qquad\bar e_N(q)=e_N(q)+\frac{q^{N+1}(N+2)}{(N+1)!\,(N+2-q)},\qquad\ch_N(y)=\sum_{n\le N}\frac{y^n}{(2n)!}.
\]
Then $e_N(q)\le e^q$ for $q\ge0$, $\ch_N(y)\le\cosh\sqrt y$ for $y\ge0$, and $e^q\le\bar e_N(q)$ for $0\le q<N+2$, since the tail of the exponential series is at most its first term times a geometric series of ratio $q/(N+2)$. So $8h_s(\rho)-U(\rho)-1$ is at least the rational number
\[
8\,e_N\Bigl(\frac{\ell^2(1-s)+s}2\Bigr)\ch_N\bigl(\ell^2s(1-s)\bigr)-8\,\bar e_N\Bigl(\frac{\ell'^2}2\Bigr)-1
\]
on $[\ell,\ell']$. Computation~\ref{comp:drift} gives, with $N=14$, a subdivision of each of the $28$ cells into subintervals with rational ends on which this number is nonnegative. This is \lname{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.drift} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the overshoot]\label{lem:overshoot}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.abs_sst_le, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.abs_rhoS_le,

\codemap{code/lean-upper-sharp/lsharp_margins.gp > code/lean-upper-sharp/out/lsharp_margins.txt, code/lower-bound-3/phase1_constants.sage > code/lower-bound-3/out/phase1_constants.out}
Let $L\ge14.5$. If $|\rho|\le a_+$, both moves of the stopped chain from $\rho$ lead to points of $[-a_+,a_+]$, and $U(\rho)\le G:=8.8\,e^{L/2}$.

\begin{proof}
If $\rho^2\ge L$ the chain stays at $\rho$. Otherwise $|\rho|<a$, and a move leads to $\rho'$ with $|\rho'|\le|\rho|\sqrt{1-s}+\sqrt s\le|\rho|+\sqrt{s(\rho)}$. If $|\rho|<2.8$ this is at most $2.8+\sqrt{0.6242}<3.6<\sqrt{14.5}\le a$. If $2.8\le|\rho|<a$, it is $|\rho|+\sqrt{5/8}\,e^{-\rho^2/4}$, and $u\mapsto u+\sqrt{5/8}\,e^{-u^2/4}$ is increasing, because its derivative is at least $1-\sqrt{5/8}\max_u\frac u2e^{-u^2/4}=1-\sqrt{5/8}\,e^{-1/2}/\sqrt2>0$; so it is at most $a+\sqrt{5/8}\,e^{-a^2/4}=a_+$. Since $U$ increases with $|\rho|$, $U(\rho)\le8e^{a_+^2/2}$ for $|\rho|\le a_+$, and $a_+^2/2=L/2+\epsilon_L$ with $\epsilon_L=\sqrt{5L/8}\,e^{-L/4}+\frac5{16}e^{-L/2}$, which is decreasing for $L\ge2$ and equals $0.08044633\ldots\le\log1.1$ at $L=14.5$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[a geometric tail]\label{lem:FL}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.lyap_add_mul_leS, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.ex_U_le, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.ex_below_pt, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.ex_below_pow}
Let $L\ge14.5$ and $G=8.8\,e^{L/2}$, and let $(\rho_i)_{i\ge0}$ be the stopped chain started at some $\rho_0$ with $|\rho_0|\le a_+$. For all integers $n\ge1$ and $j\ge0$, $\Prob(\rho_{jn}^2<L)\le(G/n)^j$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:overshoot} the chain stays in $[-a_+,a_+]$, where $0\le U\le G$. While $\rho_l^2<L$ the chain moves with the step $s(\rho_l)$, so Lemma~\ref{lem:drift} gives $\E[U(\rho_{l+1})\mid\rho_l]\ge U(\rho_l)+1$; once $\rho_l^2\ge L$ it stays. Hence $\E\,U(\rho_n)\ge U(\rho_0)+\E\,\#\{l<n:\rho_l^2<L\}$. Since the chain stays once $\rho^2\ge L$, the event $\{\rho_n^2<L\}$ implies $\rho_l^2<L$ for all $l\le n$, so $\#\{l<n:\rho_l^2<L\}\ge n\mathbf 1\{\rho_n^2<L\}$, and $n\Prob(\rho_n^2<L)\le\E\,U(\rho_n)\le G$. This holds for every starting point in $[-a_+,a_+]$, and the probability is $0$ when $\rho_0^2\ge L$; so $\Prob_\rho(\rho_n^2<L)\le(G/n)\mathbf 1\{\rho^2<L\}$ for $|\rho|\le a_+$. By the Markov property at time $jn$, $\Prob(\rho_{(j+1)n}^2<L)=\E\,\Prob_{\rho_{jn}}(\rho_n^2<L)\le(G/n)\Prob(\rho_{jn}^2<L)$, and induction on $j$ concludes.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[phase 1]\label{lem:phase1}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.prob_fail_le, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.abs_rhoS_le, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.avg_P1_nonneg}
Assume $L\ge14.5$ and $j_0\bigl(8.8\,e\,e^{L/2}+1\bigr)\le T_1-1$. Then:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(1)] $\Prob(\Succ)\ge1-e^{-j_0}$;
\item[(2)] $|\rho_{T_1}|\le a_+$, and $|\rho_{T_1}|\ge a$ on $\Succ$;
\item[(3)] for every deterministic learner, $\E\sum_{t\le T_1}(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)\ge0$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
By Lemma~\ref{lem:chain}, $\rho_1,\dots,\rho_{T_1}$ are $T_1-1$ steps of the stopped chain started at $\rho_1=\pm1$, and $|\rho_1|=1\le a_+$. (1) Let $G=8.8e^{L/2}$ and $n=\lceil eG\rceil\le eG+1$; then $j_0n\le T_1-1$. Since the chain stays once $\rho^2\ge L$, $\Prob(\rho_{T_1}^2<L)$ is at most the probability that $\rho^2<L$ after $j_0n$ steps, which is at most $(G/n)^{j_0}\le e^{-j_0}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:FL}. (2) This is Lemma~\ref{lem:overshoot} and the definition of $\Succ$. (3) Fix $t\le T_1$. The prediction $\yh_t$ is a function of $\xi_1,\dots,\xi_{t-1}$, because the features and outcomes before round $t$ and the feature $x_t$ are. The outcome $y_t=\xi_t$ is independent of them, so $\E(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)=\E\,\yh_t^2\ge0$. Sum over $t\le T_1$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Phase 2: the van Trees inequality with the past as a prior}

On $\Succ$ write $\rho=\rho_{T_1}$, $k=k_0$, $\mathcal A=\sum_{i\le k}\alpha_i^2$, and $\yh'_i=\yh_{T_1+i}$, $y'_i=y_{T_1+i}$ for $1\le i\le k$.

\begin{lemma}[the regret on a play]\label{lem:split}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.regret_succ, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.regret_fail, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.comparator_eq}
On a play of $\mathcal A_T$ in $\Succ$, for every $z\in\R$,
\[
\Reg(T)=\sum_{t\le T_1}(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)+\rho^2+\Delta(z),
\]
\[
\Delta(z)=-z^2+\sum_{i=1}^k\Bigl[(\yh'_i-y'_i)^2-\bigl((\rho+z)\alpha_i-y'_i\bigr)^2\Bigr]+(1+\mathcal A)\Bigl(\frac{\rho+\sum_i\alpha_iy'_i}{1+\mathcal A}-\rho-z\Bigr)^2 .
\]
On a play off $\Succ$, $\Reg(T)\ge\sum_{t\le T_1}(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $V=V_{T_1}$ and $Y=\sum_i\alpha_iy'_i$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:chain}, $S_{T_1}=\rho\sqrt V$, so $S_T=\sqrt V(\rho+Y)$ and $V_T=V(1+\mathcal A)$, and $\rho_T^2=(\rho+Y)^2/(1+\mathcal A)$. With $u=\rho+z$, expanding the squares gives $\sum_i[(\yh'_i-y'_i)^2-(u\alpha_i-y'_i)^2]=\sum_i(\yh_i'^2-2\yh'_iy'_i)-u^2\mathcal A+2uY$ and $(1+\mathcal A)((\rho+Y)/(1+\mathcal A)-u)^2=\rho_T^2-2u(\rho+Y)+u^2(1+\mathcal A)$, so $\rho^2+\Delta(z)=\sum_i(\yh_i'^2-2\yh'_iy'_i)+\rho_T^2+\rho^2-z^2-2u\rho+u^2$, and $u^2-2u\rho=z^2-\rho^2$. Lemma~\ref{lem:identity} concludes. Off $\Succ$, the rounds after $T_1$ have $x_t=y_t=0$, so $\rho_T=\rho_{T_1}$ and, by Lemma~\ref{lem:identity}, $\Reg(T)=\sum_{t\le T_1}(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)+\sum_{t>T_1}\yh_t^2+\rho_{T_1}^2$.
\end{proof}

For $z=Z$, the term $\Delta(Z)$ is the regret over phase 2 against the comparator $\theta^*$, minus the penalty $V(\theta^*-S_{T_1}/V_{T_1})^2=Z^2$, plus the gap between $\theta^*$ and the final least squares estimate: the past acts as a prior. The next inequality is the van Trees inequality \citep[Section~2, inequality~(3)]{GL}, in the form we need, for finitely many signs.

\begin{lemma}[van Trees inequality]\label{lem:vT}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.vanTreesG}
Let $g\colon\R\to\R$ be continuously differentiable with $g(-2)=g(2)=0$, $\int_{-2}^2g^2=1$ and $J_g=\int_{-2}^24g'^2>0$. Let $n\ge0$, and let $\alpha_l,\beta_l,c_l$ be real numbers with $c_l<1$ and $|\alpha_l+\beta_lz|\le c_l$ for $z\in[-2,2]$ and $l=1,\dots,n$. For $\eta\in\{-1,1\}^n$ and $z\in[-2,2]$ let $p_z(\eta)=\prod_l\frac12\bigl(1+\eta_l(\alpha_l+\beta_lz)\bigr)$. Then for every function $\psi\colon\{-1,1\}^n\to\R$,
\[
\int_{-2}^2\sum_{\eta}g(z)^2\,p_z(\eta)\,\bigl(\psi(\eta)-z\bigr)^2dz\ge\frac1{J_g+\sum_l\beta_l^2/(1-c_l^2)}.
\]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Let $q(z,\eta)=g(z)^2p_z(\eta)$; it is positive on $(-2,2)$ wherever $g\ne0$, and $p_z(\eta)>0$ since $c_l<1$. For each $\eta$, integration by parts gives $\int_{-2}^2(\psi(\eta)-z)\,\partial_zq\,dz=\int_{-2}^2q\,dz$, the boundary terms vanishing because $g(\pm2)=0$; summing over $\eta$, and since $\sum_\eta p_z(\eta)=1$ and $\int g^2=1$, $\sum_\eta\int(\psi-z)\,\partial_zq\,dz=1$. Now $\partial_zq=g\,p_z(\eta)\bigl(g\,\varsigma+2g'\bigr)$ with the score $\varsigma(z,\eta)=\sum_l\eta_l\beta_l/(1+\eta_l(\alpha_l+\beta_lz))$. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality for the measure $p_z(\eta)\,dz$ times counting measure,
\[
1\le\Bigl[\sum_\eta\int g^2p_z(\eta)(\psi-z)^2dz\Bigr]\Bigl[\sum_\eta\int p_z(\eta)\bigl(g\,\varsigma+2g'\bigr)^2dz\Bigr].
\]
Under $p_z$ the signs are independent, and $\eta_l\beta_l/(1+\eta_l(\alpha_l+\beta_lz))$ has mean $0$ and second moment $\beta_l^2/(1-(\alpha_l+\beta_lz)^2)\le\beta_l^2/(1-c_l^2)$. So $\sum_\eta p_z\varsigma=0$ and $\sum_\eta p_z\varsigma^2\le\sum_l\beta_l^2/(1-c_l^2)$, and the second factor is at most $\int\bigl(g^2\sum_l\beta_l^2/(1-c_l^2)+4g'^2\bigr)dz=\sum_l\beta_l^2/(1-c_l^2)+J_g$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[the prior]\label{lem:prior}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.integral_gP_sq, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.integral_gP_deriv_sq, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.integral_sq_gP_sq, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.gP_two, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.gP_neg_two}
\codemap{code/lower-bound-2/gp/constants.gp > code/lower-bound-2/out/constants.txt, code/lower-bound-3/phase2.sage > code/lower-bound-3/out/phase2.out}
The function $g(z)=\cos(\pi z/4)/\sqrt2$ is continuously differentiable on $\R$, with $g(\pm2)=0$, $\int_{-2}^2g^2=1$, $\int_{-2}^24g'^2=J=\pi^2/4$ and $\int_{-2}^2z^2g(z)^2\,dz=\frac43-\frac8{\pi^2}$. Its square is the density $f_Z$ of $Z$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
With $z=2u$, $\int_{-2}^2g^2=\int_{-1}^1\cos^2(\pi u/2)\,du=1$. Next $g'(z)=-\frac\pi{4\sqrt2}\sin(\pi z/4)$, so $\int_{-2}^24g'^2=\frac{\pi^2}8\int_{-2}^2\sin^2(\pi z/4)\,dz=\frac{\pi^2}4$. For the second moment, $\int_{-2}^2z^2g^2=\frac14\int_{-2}^2z^2(1+\cos(\pi z/2))\,dz=\frac14\bigl(\frac{16}3-\frac{32}{\pi^2}\bigr)$, since $\int_{-1}^1u^2\cos(\pi u)\,du=-\frac2\pi\int_{-1}^1u\sin(\pi u)\,du=-\frac4{\pi^2}$.
\end{proof}

\begin{lemma}[phase 2]\label{lem:phase2}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.phase2S, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.beta_ge}
\mapnote{Lean proves the first claim for every radius $r\ge|\rho|+2$.}
\codemap{code/lower-bound/phase2_check.gp > code/lower-bound/phase2_check.out, code/lower-bound-2/gp/phase2.gp > code/lower-bound-2/out/phase2.txt, code/lower-bound-3/phase2.sage > code/lower-bound-3/out/phase2.out}
\mapnote{the programs test the lemma at sample points; the proof uses no computation}
Fix phase-1 signs on $\Succ$, and let $\rho$, $k$, $r$ and $\Delta$ be as above. Let $\Info_0=J$, $\Info_i=\Info_{i-1}+\alpha_i^2/(1-\nu_i^2)$ for $1\le i\le k$. For every deterministic learner, $\E[\Delta(Z)\mid\xi_1,\dots,\xi_{T_1}]\ge\beta(\rho,k)$, where
\[
\beta(\rho,k)=\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\alpha_i^2}{\Info_{i-1}}-\E Z^2+\frac{1+\mathcal A}{\Info_k};
\]
and if $k\ge\pi^2e^2r^2$, then $\beta(\rho,k)\ge\underline\beta(\rho,k):=\log(k/r^2)-c_1-\log(k)/(2k)-1/k$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Condition on the phase-1 signs throughout. Then $\rho$, $r$ and all phase-2 features are fixed numbers, $Z$ has the density $g^2$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:prior}, and given $Z=z$ the outcomes $y'_1,\dots,y'_k$ are independent signs with $\Prob(y'_l=1)=\frac12(1+(\rho+z)\alpha_l)$, the model of Lemma~\ref{lem:vT} with $\alpha_l\rho$ and $\alpha_l$ in place of $\alpha_l$ and $\beta_l$, and $c_l=\nu_l<1$, since $|(\rho+z)\alpha_l|\le r\alpha_l=\nu_l$.

(i) Given $Z$ and $y'_1,\dots,y'_{i-1}$, the prediction $\yh'_i$ is fixed and $y'_i$ has mean $(\rho+Z)\alpha_i$, so the conditional mean of $(\yh'_i-y'_i)^2-((\rho+Z)\alpha_i-y'_i)^2$ is $(\yh'_i-(\rho+Z)\alpha_i)^2$. Also $\E Z^2=\frac43-\frac8{\pi^2}$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:prior}.

(ii) Round $T_1+i$. The prediction $\yh'_i$ is a function of $y'_1,\dots,y'_{i-1}$; write $\yh'_i=\alpha_i(\psi+\rho)$, which defines $\psi$, as $\alpha_i>0$. Then $(\yh'_i-(\rho+Z)\alpha_i)^2=\alpha_i^2(\psi-Z)^2$, and Lemma~\ref{lem:vT} for the first $i-1$ outcomes, with $J_g=J$, gives $\E(\psi-Z)^2\ge1/(J+\sum_{l<i}\alpha_l^2/(1-\nu_l^2))=1/\Info_{i-1}$. Hence $\E(\yh'_i-(\rho+Z)\alpha_i)^2\ge\alpha_i^2/\Info_{i-1}$.

(iii) The number $(\rho+\sum_i\alpha_iy'_i)/(1+\mathcal A)-\rho$ is a function of the $k$ outcomes, and Lemma~\ref{lem:vT} with all of them gives $\E[(1+\mathcal A)(\dots-\rho-Z)^2]\ge(1+\mathcal A)/\Info_k$.

Summing (i) to (iii) gives the first claim.

(iv) Let $\omega_i=\log(\Info_i/J)$, so $\omega_0=0$. Since $\alpha_i^2=(1-\nu_i^2)(\Info_i-\Info_{i-1})$ and $u\ge\log(1+u)$, we have $\alpha_i^2/\Info_{i-1}\ge(1-\nu_i^2)(\omega_i-\omega_{i-1})$. With $\nu_i^2=i/(2k)$, summation by parts gives
\[
\sum_{i=1}^k\frac{\alpha_i^2}{\Info_{i-1}}\ge\frac12\,\omega_k+\frac1{2k}\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\omega_i .
\]
Since $\Info_i\ge J+\sum_{l\le i}\alpha_l^2=J(1+\mu i(i+1))$ with $\mu=1/(\pi^2r^2k)$, we have $\omega_i\ge\log(1+\mu i^2)\ge0$. Put $\Lambda=\log(\mu k^2)=\log(k/(\pi^2r^2))$, which is at most $\log k$. Then $\frac12\omega_k\ge\Lambda/2$, and, $\log(1+\mu x^2)$ being increasing in $x\ge0$,
\[
\frac1{2k}\sum_{i=1}^{k-1}\log(1+\mu i^2)\ge\frac1{2k}\int_0^{k-1}\log(\mu x^2)\,dx=\frac{k-1}{2k}\Bigl(\log\bigl(\mu(k-1)^2\bigr)-2\Bigr).
\]
Now $\log(\mu(k-1)^2)=\Lambda+2\log(1-1/k)\ge\Lambda-2/(k-1)$, so the right side is at least $\frac{k-1}{2k}(\Lambda-2)-\frac1k\ge\frac{\Lambda-2}2-\frac\Lambda{2k}-\frac1k$. Hence $\sum_i\alpha_i^2/\Info_{i-1}\ge\Lambda-1-\log(k)/(2k)-1/k$. For the last term, $\Info_k\le J+2\mathcal A$ because $\nu_i^2\le1/2$, and $\mathcal A=(k+1)/(4r^2)$, so
\[
\frac{1+\mathcal A}{\Info_k}\ge\frac{1+\mathcal A}{J+2\mathcal A}=\frac12-\frac{J/2-1}{J+2\mathcal A}\ge\frac12-\frac{J-2}{4\mathcal A}\ge\frac12-\frac{J-2}{\pi^2e^2},
\]
the last step because $4\mathcal A=(k+1)/r^2\ge\pi^2e^2$ when $k\ge\pi^2e^2r^2$; this is the only use of that condition. Adding, $\beta(\rho,k)\ge\log(k/r^2)-\log(\pi^2)-1-\E Z^2+\frac12-\frac{J-2}{\pi^2e^2}-\frac{\log k}{2k}-\frac1k=\underline\beta(\rho,k)$.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}}

The proof uses three side conditions on $T$:
\begin{enumerate}
\item[(C1)] $L\ge14.5$;
\item[(C2)] $j_0(8.8\,e\,e^{L/2}+1)\le T_1-1$;
\item[(C3)] $k_0\ge\pi^2e^2(a_++2)^2$.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:side}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.level_ge, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.C2, RegretKappa.LowerSharp.C3}
\codemap{code/lean-upper-sharp/lsharp_margins.gp > code/lean-upper-sharp/out/lsharp_margins.txt, code/lower-bound/total_bound.gp > code/lower-bound/total_bound.out, code/lower-bound-3/total_bound.sage > code/lower-bound-3/out/total_bound.out}
Conditions (C1), (C2) and (C3) hold for every integer $T\ge T_0=355713$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(C1) If $j_0\le4$, then $L\ge2\log(T_0/(80e))\ge14.5$, since $T_0\ge80\cdot4446\ge80\,e^{33/4}$; at $T_0$, where $j_0=4$, $L=14.799705\ldots$. If $j_0=j\ge5$, then $\log(3\log T)>j-1$ by the definition of $j_0$, so $2\log T>\frac23e^{j-1}$, and $L=2\log T-2\log(20ej)>\frac23e^{j-1}-2\log(20ej)$, which is increasing in $j\ge5$ and equals $25.188426\ldots$ at $j=5$. (C2) By the definition of $L$, $8.8\,e\,e^{L/2}j_0=0.44\,T$, and $0.44\,T+j_0\le T/2-3/2\le T_1-1$ because $0.06\,T\ge j_0+3/2$ for $T\ge T_0$, as $j_0<\log(3\log T)+1\le\log\log T+2.1$. As $\log T\le\log T_0+(T-T_0)/T_0<13+(T-T_0)/T_0$, $74(4\log T+18)\le5180+296(T-T_0)/T_0\le T_0/2+(T-T_0)/2=T/2\le k_0$.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}]
(a) Fix a deterministic learner, and let $P_1=\sum_{t\le T_1}(\yh_t^2-2\yh_ty_t)$. By Lemma~\ref{lem:split} with $z=Z$, $\Reg(T)=P_1+\rho_{T_1}^2+\Delta(Z)$ on $\Succ$, and $\Reg(T)\ge P_1$ off $\Succ$. Conditioning on the phase-1 signs and applying Lemma~\ref{lem:phase2} on $\Succ$,
\[
\E\,\Reg(T)\ge\E P_1+\E\bigl[\mathbf 1_\Succ\bigl(\rho_{T_1}^2+\beta(\rho_{T_1},k_0)\bigr)\bigr],
\]
and $\E P_1\ge0$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:phase1}(3), which applies by (C1) and (C2). On $\Succ$, $a\le|\rho_{T_1}|\le a_+$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:phase1}(2), so $r\le a_++2$ and (C3) makes the bound $\underline\beta$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:phase2} valid. The function $u\mapsto u^2+\log(k_0/(u+2)^2)$ is nondecreasing on $[1,\infty)$, with derivative $2u-2/(u+2)>0$, and $a\ge1$; hence on $\Succ$,
\[
\rho_{T_1}^2+\underline\beta(\rho_{T_1},k_0)\ge B_0:=L+\log\frac{k_0}{(a+2)^2}-c_1-\frac{\log k_0}{2k_0}-\frac1{k_0},
\]
and, since $B_0\ge0$ (shown below), $\E\,\Reg(T)\ge\Prob(\Succ)B_0\ge(1-e^{-j_0})B_0=b(T)$ by Lemma~\ref{lem:phase1}(1). The expectation is the weighted sum over the finitely many plays of $\mathcal A_T$, whose features are nonnegative and whose outcomes lie in $\{-1,0,1\}$, which proves (a).

(b) Here $L=2\log T-2\log(20e)-2\log j_0$ with $j_0\le\log\log T+2.1$, as in the proof of (C2); $\log k_0\ge\log(T/2)$; $2\log(a+2)=\log L+2\log(1+2/a)\le\log\log T+\log2+0.85$, since $L\le2\log T$ and $2\log(1+2/\sqrt{14.5})=0.84428484\ldots$; $\log(k_0)/(2k_0)+1/k_0\le10^{-4}$; and $c_1\le3.33$. Collecting, and since $2\log(20e)+2\log2+0.85+3.33+10^{-4}\le13.59$,
\[
B_0\ge3\log T-\log\log T-2\log(\log\log T+2.1)-13.6 .
\]
Next, $z\mapsto z-2\log(z+2.1)$ is increasing for $z>-0.1$ and equals $-0.5248577\ldots$ at $z=\log\log T_0=2.5480285\ldots$, so for $T\ge T_0$ the right side is at least $3\log T-2\log\log T-14.2$, which is positive, because $3x-2\log x$ increases for $x\ge1$ and exceeds $14.2$ at $x=12<\log T_0$; in particular $B_0\ge0$. Since $e^{-j_0}\le1/(3\log T)$ and $B_0\le3\log T$, $b(T)\ge B_0-1$, which gives the first form, and the same monotonicity, with $-0.5248577\ldots>-0.6$, gives the second.

(c) Let $B>0$ and let $\mathcal L$ be a deterministic learner. By (a), applied to the learner $\mathcal L^{1/B}$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:scaling}, some play $(x^i,y^i)$ has $\Reg(x^i,y^i)\ge b(T)$ for $\mathcal L^{1/B}$, since a weighted average with weights of sum $1$ is at most the largest term. Since $(\mathcal L^{1/B})^B=\mathcal L$, Lemma~\ref{lem:scaling} gives that the regret of $\mathcal L$ on $(x^i,By^i)$ is $B^2$ times that, at least $B^2b(T)$, and the outcomes $By^i_t$ lie in $[-B,B]$. This is \lname{RegretKappa.LowerSharp.lowerSharpBound} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\section{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}; bounded features and randomized learners}\label{sec:proofmain}

This section assembles Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, shows that its lower bound holds with features in $[0,1]$ and outcomes in $\{-B,0,B\}$ and against randomized learners, and compares it with the case of features known in advance.

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}]
Let $B>0$ and $T\ge T_0$. The first inequality is Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(b) multiplied by $B^2$. By Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(c), every deterministic learner has a play with outcomes in $[-B,B]$ and $\Reg(T)\ge B^2b(T)$, so the supremum in \eqref{eq:minimax} is at least $B^2b(T)$ for every learner, and $\Regs_T(B)\ge B^2b(T)$. The learner of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} for outcomes in $[-B,B]$ has $\Reg(T)\le B^2u(T)=B^2U(T)$ on every play, so $\Regs_T(B)\le B^2U(T)$. Finally $U(T)/\log T\to3$ by the second form of $u(T)$ in Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}, and $(3\log T-2\log\log T-15.2)/\log T\to3$; dividing by $B^2\log T$ gives the limit. This is \lname{RegretKappa.main} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

The learner of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} uses $B$ and $T$. The lower bound holds against every learner, so in particular for learners that know neither $B$ nor $T$.

\subsection{Bounded features and randomized learners}
The adversary $\mathcal A_T$ plays features that grow geometrically in phase 1. Since its plays are finitely many, its features can be scaled into $[0,1]$ at no cost, and since it ignores the predictions, its bound extends to randomized learners. For a set $\mathcal Y\subseteq[-B,B]$ let $\Regs_T(B;[0,1],\mathcal Y)$ be the minimax regret \eqref{eq:minimax} with the supremum restricted to the plays with features in $[0,1]$ and outcomes in $\mathcal Y$.

\begin{corollary}\label{cor:bounded}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.mainBoundedFeatures, RegretKappa.Corollaries.boundedFeatures_of_mixture, RegretKappa.Corollaries.le_advRegret}
\mapnote{Lean states it as \lname{RegretKappa.MainBoundedFeatures}, with the two minimax regrets \lname{RegretKappa.Corollaries.minimaxRegretBF} and \lname{RegretKappa.Corollaries.minimaxRegretBFI} in the extended real numbers, for randomized learners on probability spaces in every universe.}
Let $B>0$ and $T\ge T_0$. There is an adversary that ignores the predictions and plays finitely many plays $(x^i,y^i)$, with probabilities $w_i$, with features in $[0,1]$ and outcomes in $\{-B,0,B\}$, against which every randomized learner has expected regret at least $B^2b(T)$. For deterministic learners,
\begin{align*}
B^2\bigl(3\log T-2\log\log T-15.2\bigr)\le B^2b(T)&\le\Regs_T\bigl(B;[0,1],\{-B,0,B\}\bigr)\\
&\le\Regs_T\bigl(B;[0,1],[-B,B]\bigr)\le B^2\,U(T).
\end{align*}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Let $(x^i,y^i,w_i)$ be the adversary of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(a), and $M=1+\sum_{i,t}x^i_t$, which depends on $T$ only. The new adversary plays $(x^i/M,By^i)$ with probability $w_i$; its features lie in $[0,1]$, since $x^i_t\ge0$, and its outcomes in $\{-B,0,B\}$. For a deterministic learner $\mathcal L$, let $\mathcal L'$ predict $\frac1B\mathcal L_t(x_1/M,\dots,x_t/M;By_1,\dots,By_{t-1})$; this is a learner, because $M$ depends on $T$ only. On $(x^i,y^i)$ its predictions are $1/B$ times those of $\mathcal L$ on $(x^i/M,By^i)$, and $\inf_\theta\sum_t(\theta x^i_t/M-By^i_t)^2=B^2\inf_\theta\sum_t(\theta x^i_t-y^i_t)^2$ by the substitution $\theta\mapsto MB\theta$; so the regret of $\mathcal L$ on $(x^i/M,By^i)$ is $B^2$ times the regret of $\mathcal L'$ on $(x^i,y^i)$, and $\sum_iw_i\Reg_{\mathcal L}(x^i/M,By^i)\ge B^2b(T)$ by Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(a). For a randomized learner, this holds for each $\mathcal L^\omega$; adding $\sum_iw_i\sum_t(By^i_t)^2$ and integrating against the probability $\mu$ gives the first claim. In the chain, the first inequality is Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(b); the second holds because, for each deterministic learner, some play of the new adversary has regret at least the weighted average; the third because $\{-B,0,B\}\subseteq[-B,B]$; and the fourth by the learner of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}. This is \lname{RegretKappa.mainBoundedFeatures} in the formalization.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[randomized learners on one play]\label{rem:rand}
\leanmap{RegretKappa.Corollaries.randLowerBound}
\mapnote{Lean states it as \lname{RegretKappa.Corollaries.RandLowerBound}; the readings without measurability are refuted by \lname{RegretKappa.CorollariesCheck.V42b.not_randLowerBound}, \lname{RegretKappa.CorollariesCheck.not_randLowerBound} and \lname{RegretKappa.CorollariesCheck.V51c.not_randPlay}.}
For every $B>0$ and $\varepsilon>0$ there is $T_\varepsilon$ such that for every $T\ge T_\varepsilon$, every randomized learner has a play with outcomes in $[-B,B]$ on which its expected regret is at least $(3-\varepsilon)B^2\log T$. By the measurability in the definition of a randomized learner, its expected regret against an adversary with finitely many plays is the weighted average of its expected regrets on the plays, so this follows from Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded}, as $b(T)\ge(3-\varepsilon)\log T$ for large $T$. The measurability cannot be dropped: without it the expected regret is not defined, and under the usual conventions that give a value to the integral of a function that is not measurable, the Bochner integral and the lower integral, the statement fails (Section~\ref{subsec:lean}).
\end{remark}

When the features are known to the learner from the start, the minimax regret in dimension one lies between $B^2(\log T-\log\log T-3)$ for $T\ge8$, with features in $[0,1]$ \citep[Theorem~4]{GGHS}, and $B^2(\log(1+T)+1)$, for all real features \citep[Theorem~7]{GGHS}. By Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded}, revealing the features one at a time raises the constant from $1$ to $3$. In dimension one this answers the first alternative of Question~\ref{q:GGHS}: the lower bound becomes larger, and no bound $B^2\log T+O(1)$ holds uniformly over the comparators and over bounded features revealed on the fly.

\section{Further remarks}\label{sec:remarks}

\emph{The additive constants.} Theorem~\ref{thm:main} leaves a gap of order $\log\log T$ between the two bounds. In the lower bound, the term $-\log\log T$ of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(b) comes from the radius $r=|\rho_{T_1}|+2$ of phase 2, through $\log(k/r^2)$ in Lemma~\ref{lem:phase2} with $r^2\approx2\log T$, and the term $-2\log(\log\log T+2.1)$ from the factor $j_0$ in the level $L=2\log(T/(20e\,j_0))$, which makes room in phase 1 for the $j_0$ blocks of about $eG$ rounds in the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:phase1}(1). A second order term of order $\log\log T$ is not specific to features revealed on the fly: for the constant feature $x_t=1$, known in advance, the minimax regret is $B^2(\log T-\log\log T+O(\log\log T/\log T))$ \citep[Corollary~1 and Lemma~3, for their loss, which is half of ours]{TW00}. Whether $\Regs_T(1)-3\log T$ is bounded is open.

\emph{Unknown $B$ or $T$.} The learner of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper} uses $B$ and $T$. Whether a learner that knows neither, the setting of \citet{GGHS}, has regret $(3+o(1))B^2\log T$ on every play is open; the lower bound of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} applies to it unchanged.

\emph{Higher dimension.} In dimension $d\ge2$ there is no such constant: the doubly uniform regret is linear in $T$ \citep[Theorem~7]{CQRZ}.

\section{Computations and formal proof}\label{sec:checks}

This section describes how the formal proof and the computations were run and checked, and what each check relies on. The repository \citep{repo} maps every numbered statement to its formal declarations and to its scripts and recorded outputs, and gives the commands that rerun each check.

\subsection{Software and machine}\label{subsec:machine}
All checks ran on one machine, an AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (24 threads) under Linux, shared with other work, so the running times below are indications. The formal proof is checked by Lean~4.34.1 \citep{Lean4} with Mathlib \citep{Mathlib} at its release v4.34.1. The second computations use the exact rational arithmetic of PARI/GP~2.17.4 \citep{PARI} and of SageMath~10.9 \citep{Sage}, and the ball arithmetic of Arb \citep{Arb} in SageMath.

\subsection{The formal proof}\label{subsec:lean}
The formal statement defines a deterministic learner for the horizon $T$ as a sequence of maps from $x_1,\dots,x_t$ and $y_1,\dots,y_{t-1}$ to a prediction, the regret by \eqref{eq:regret}, and the minimax regret by \eqref{eq:minimax}, as an infimum of suprema in the extended real numbers, so that both are the true ones; it defines randomized learners and their expected regret as in Section~\ref{sec:setting}, the expectation being taken in the extended real numbers. On these definitions it states Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, Theorems~\ref{thm:upper} and~\ref{thm:lower}, Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded} and Remark~\ref{rem:rand}, with the constants of the paper. Lean proves all of them with no hypothesis and only the three standard axioms of Lean (propositional extensionality, the axiom of choice and the soundness of quotients), with no unproved step and no call to compiled code. Every numerical step of the proofs is an inequality between rational numbers, which Lean checks in exact arithmetic from rational bounds, proved in Lean, of the constants involved ($e$, $\pi$, square roots, logarithms and $K_0$); the one larger computation is the drift certificate of Computation~\ref{comp:drift}, which Lean's kernel evaluates.

Formal statements of the same results were written separately from the text of the paper and compared with ours in Lean. Lean proves the separate statements of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}, of Theorem~\ref{thm:upper}, of parts (a) and (c) of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower} and of Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded} equivalent to ours, the last one for learners randomized on probability spaces in the lowest universe of Lean's types, and ours of Theorem~\ref{thm:lower}(b) implies the separate one. For the limit alone there are two more: Lean proves that the halves and the limit of the first are each equivalent to ours; the second took the infimum and the suprema in the real numbers, where the supremum of a set that is not bounded above is $0$ by convention, and Lean proves that its minimax regret is $0$ for every $T$ and $B\ge0$, so that its lower half is false, and that the same definitions with the extended real numbers are equivalent to ours. For Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded} and Remark~\ref{rem:rand}, the readings in which the integrated function is measurable are equivalent to ours, in the lowest universe, or implied by them; the readings in which it need not be, through the trivial $\sigma$-algebra on the learners, the Bochner integral or the lower integral, are false, and Lean proves it. This is why the definition of Section~\ref{sec:setting} asks for measurability.

The continuous integration of the repository builds the formal proof, fails on any axiom other than these three, and runs Comparator \citep{Comparator} on eleven theorems: those of Theorems~\ref{thm:main}, \ref{thm:upper} and~\ref{thm:lower}, of Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded} and of Remark~\ref{rem:rand}, and the three of the first formal proof of the limit (its two halves and the limit), described below. Comparator checks that they prove the statements as written again, with the definitions they use, in separate files that import only Mathlib and one another, and replays their proofs in nanoda \citep{nanoda}, a type checker for Lean written separately from Lean's kernel. Building the formal proof takes about $8$ minutes on four cores of the machine of Section~\ref{subsec:machine} once Mathlib is built, and the Comparator run about $2$ minutes.

\emph{Where the routes differ.} The formal proof follows the paper, with these differences. It takes the limit of Theorem~\ref{thm:main} from a first formal proof of the limit with cruder constants, in place of the bounds of the theorem: a learner of the form \eqref{eq:learner}, with the increment $120$ in place of $3$ and other constants $C$ and $\lambda_0$, which has $\Reg(T)\le3\log T+2\log962$ on every play with outcomes in $[-1,1]$, and an adversary with the move rule $e^{-\rho^2/2}/4$ and the prior density $\frac{15}{512}(4-z^2)^2$, which forces $3\log T-6\log\log T-4$ for $\log T\ge256$. It proves Remark~\ref{rem:rand} from this adversary, in place of Corollary~\ref{cor:bounded}. It works with $\Gamma/2$ in place of $\Gamma$. It proves Lemma~\ref{lem:small} without expanding $\cosh$ in a series, from polynomial bounds on $\cosh$ with a remainder, which lead to the same quartic $f$. In part (iv) of the proof of Lemma~\ref{lem:phase2} it bounds $\sum_{1\le i<k}\log(i/k)$ through $k^k/k!\le e^k$ in place of the integral of $\log$.

\emph{Coverage.} For the upper bound, two SageMath programs in Arb ball arithmetic each check items (1), (2), (4) and (5) of Computation~\ref{comp:upper} and compute the values quoted in Section~\ref{sec:upper}, and they agree; a PARI/GP program recomputes item (3), and both SageMath programs check the bound $2.7053$ of Lemma~\ref{lem:large} on another subdivision, twenty equal pieces of $[0,(1+\sqrt3)/\sqrt2]$. \emph{Negative controls.} The programs were run on corrupted inputs, which they must reject, and they do. Both PARI/GP drift programs reject the tables in which $s_{16}$ is replaced by $0.1852$ or $s_{27}$ by $0.0050$. The SageMath program of the lower bound rejects $19$ corrupted inputs, among them $s_{16}=0.1000$, the factor $7$ in place of $8$ in $U$, $T_0-1$ in place of $T_0$, and $15.0$ in place of $15.2$. At $T_0-1$ what it rejects is its own uniform lower bound $2\log T-2\log(20e)-2\log(\log(3\log T)+1)$ of $L$, which is $14.4999998\ldots$ there, while $L=14.7997\ldots$. So this control does not show that the proof needs $T\ge T_0$: the proof of (C1) in Lemma~\ref{lem:side} uses only $T\ge80e^{33/4}$. The run of the upper bound programs rejects a failing check, a crashing check and a forged output. On the formal side, Comparator rejects a challenge in which the term $-1/k_0$ of $b(T)$ is replaced by $-2/k_0$.

\emph{Reruns.} The programs of Table~\ref{tab:checks}, except the third program of the lower bound, and three PARI/GP programs of tests (the constants of Section~\ref{sec:upper} in $60$ digits and two tests of Lemma~\ref{lem:phase2}) were each run twice more; every run reproduced the recorded output line for line, apart from the running times and dates printed in some lines and the lines that hold only a running time. The continuous integration of the repository reruns the first program of the upper bound and the third program of the lower bound, with their negative controls, and compares the outputs of their checks with the recorded ones, with the running times and dates masked.
